## The Problem

Current GUI agents are expensive, and when you look at how they actually work, the inefficiency becomes obvious. Claude Computer Use, OpenAI Operator, whatever Google is cooking—they all follow the same pattern: screenshot goes in, massive MLLM forward pass happens, action comes out, and this repeats for every single step with no memory of how the interface behaves, no model of what happens when you click, just pattern matching at scale.

Apple's UI-JEPA showed there's another way entirely. They achieved $50.5\times$ compute reduction versus GPT-4 Turbo on intent prediction, not by shrinking the model or using fewer parameters, but by predicting in latent space instead of pixel or token space, which turns out to be a fundamentally different approach.

So I tried to build one, specifically for UI automation, over a single weekend, using 45 training samples from a YouTube tutorial I found.

It worked better than it should have.

---

## What Even Is a World Model

The idea is old, older than most of the current deep learning boom, and Schmidhuber wrote about this back in 1990, proposing two networks where one learns to predict what happens and the other uses those predictions to plan, with the prediction error becoming curiosity that drives agents to explore what they don't understand.

Then it sat there, largely ignored, for 28 years until deep learning finally made it practical.

**The evolution, quickly:**

| Year | What Happened | Why It Matters |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| 2018 | Ha & Schmidhuber's "World Models" | Agent dreams inside hallucinated RNN environment |
| 2018 | PlaNet (Hafner) | RSSM—recurrent state space model for planning |
| 2019 | DreamerV1 | Actually learns behaviors by backpropagating through imagined trajectories |
| 2020 | DreamerV2 | Categorical latents, beats humans on Atari |
| 2022 | TD-MPC | Decoder-free implicit world model |
| 2023 | DreamerV3 | One algorithm, 150+ tasks, Minecraft diamonds from scratch. Published in *Nature* 2025. |
| 2023 | TD-MPC2 | 317M parameters, 80 tasks, no hyperparameter tuning |

DreamerV3 is the big one, the result that made people pay attention, because the same architecture learns platformer physics, Minecraft resource constraints, and robot locomotion without modification, which demonstrates the power of learning dynamics in latent space rather than memorizing specific environments.

Then LeCun proposed JEPA, which stands for Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture, with a core insight that sounds simple but changes everything: predict representations, not pixels.

**JEPA timeline:**

| Year | Paper | The Gist |
|------|-------|----------|
| 2022 | LeCun's "Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" | 6-module architecture proposal |
| 2023 | I-JEPA (Meta) | First implementation. ViT-H on ImageNet in under 72 hours. No augmentations. |
| 2023 | MC-JEPA | Motion + content together |
| 2024 | V-JEPA (Meta) | Video. 81.9% Kinetics-400, 72.2% SSv2. Feature prediction only. |
| 2024 | UI-JEPA (Apple) | UI intent prediction. 50.5x less compute than GPT-4 Turbo. |
| 2024 | seq-JEPA | Autoregressive variant |
| 2025 | V-JEPA 2 | 1M+ hours video. Zero-shot robot control via MPC in latent space. |
| 2025 | SALT (Apple) | Frozen teacher instead of EMA. More efficient. |

The pattern: everyone is moving from pixels to representations.

## How V-JEPA Actually Works (The Technical Part)

### Tubelet Tokenization

Video isn't just a sequence of images, it has temporal structure that matters, and V-JEPA processes this using 3D tubelets of $2 \text{ frames} \times 16 \times 16 \text{ pixels}$ each.

For a 16-frame clip at $256 \times 256$ resolution, the math works out as follows:

$$
N_{\text{spatial}} = \left(\frac{256}{16}\right)^2 = 256 \text{ per frame pair}
$$

$$
N_{\text{temporal}} = \frac{16}{2} = 8
$$

$$
N_{\text{tokens}} = N_{\text{spatial}} \times N_{\text{temporal}} = 256 \times 8 = 2{,}048
$$

Each token embeds to $d = 1{,}024$ dimensions when using ViT-L, giving you that $[1, 2048, 1024]$ tensor shape you see in the code, and every single token captures a spatiotemporal slice containing motion and change, not just static appearance.

### The Masking Strategy (This Is Critical)

V-JEPA masks approximately $\sim 90\%$ of tokens, but crucially, not randomly, because that would make the task trivial and the model wouldn't learn meaningful representations.

Instead, it uses multi-block masking with three components working together: short-range small blocks that force fine-grained motion understanding, long-range large tube blocks that force global structure and long-term dependencies, and a random mix of these that prevents the model from finding shortcuts.

By hiding most of the input and forcing prediction from the remaining context, the model learns what's actually predictable—object positions, motion trajectories, physical dynamics—while learning to ignore what's just noise, like exact pixel values, lighting variations, and compression artifacts.

### Three Modules

**Context encoder** processes the visible patches, which represent only about $\sim 10\%$ of the total input after masking.

**Target encoder** is an exponential moving average of the context encoder, updated slowly with $\tau \approx 0.996\text{--}0.999$ rather than through gradients, which prevents representation collapse and maintains stable training targets throughout the process. The EMA update rule:

$$
\bar{\theta} \leftarrow \tau \cdot \bar{\theta} + (1 - \tau) \cdot \theta
$$

**Predictor** is a lightweight transformer with 12 layers and 384 dimensions for V-JEPA 1, or 24 layers and 1024 dimensions for V-JEPA 2-AC, which takes context embeddings and mask positions as input and predicts the corresponding target embeddings.

### The Loss

Not pixels. $L_1$ distance in embedding space:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \left\| \hat{z}_t - z_t^{\text{target}} \right\|_1
$$

where $\hat{z}_t$ is the predictor's output and $z_t^{\text{target}}$ comes from the EMA target encoder. This is the key. The model learns high-influence features (things that actually matter for prediction) and ignores surface noise. Apple's ICLR 2025 paper on implicit bias confirms this mechanistically: latent prediction biases toward features with high regression coefficients.

## Why This Matters for UI

### The Two Camps

    style WIN fill:#10b981,color:#fff,stroke:#059669

**DOM-based** approaches parse the HTML or accessibility tree, which makes them fast and precise but also brittle, breaking whenever they encounter custom components, canvas-based UIs, or Shadow DOM that doesn't follow standard patterns.

**Pixel-based** approaches take a screenshot, feed it to an MLLM, and get an action back, which is general but expensive, requiring every step to process millions of pixels with no learned dynamics from previous steps.

World models offer a third path entirely: learning interface dynamics in compressed latent space, where the model captures how the UI behaves rather than just what it looks like.

When you click "Create Deal," you don't need every pixel of the resulting form rendered perfectly, you just need to know that a form appeared, that certain fields are now active, that the state transitioned from list view to creation view—semantic facts, not pixel values.

### The Efficiency Argument

Apple's numbers on IIW dataset:

| Metric | UI-JEPA | GPT-4 Turbo | Claude 3.5 Sonnet |
|--------|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Intent similarity | Baseline | -10.0% | -7.2% |
| Compute cost | $1\times$ | $50.5\times$ | $\sim 50\times$ |
| Latency | $1\times$ | $6.6\times$ worse | $\sim 6\times$ worse |

$50\times$ less compute, and it actually performs better. The caveat here is important: UI-JEPA does intent understanding, which is figuring out what the user is doing, not action execution, which is taking over and doing things itself, and these are fundamentally different problems that require different approaches.

## The Experiment

### Hypothesis

Can a simple transformer learn to predict UI states in V-JEPA latent space with minimal data (45 samples), then decode back to recognizable visuals?

The bet: V-JEPA embeddings already know what buttons and forms look like. We just need to learn the dynamics for this specific interface.

### Data

I used one YouTube tutorial on Zoho CRM deal creation, downloaded it in 1080p, chopped it into 2-second clips using FFmpeg, and ended up with 45 training samples total, which is intentionally minimal to test whether this approach works with limited data.

For annotation, I used Llama Scout to label each transition with action type and coordinates, which gave me supervised labels without manual effort.

### Training

Loss function:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \text{MSE}(\hat{z}, z) + \lambda \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\hat{z} \cdot z}{\|\hat{z}\| \, \|z\|}\right)
$$

where $\lambda = 0.1$ balances the two terms. MSE gets numerical proximity, making sure the predicted embedding is close to the target in absolute terms, while cosine similarity gets semantic direction, ensuring the prediction points in the same conceptual direction, and both matter for different reasons.

Hyperparameters were straightforward: AdamW optimizer with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$ and cosine decay, running for 50 epochs with batch size 4, which is small but necessary given the tiny dataset.

### Results

| Metric | Start | End | Change |
|--------|-------|-----|--------|
| Train loss | 12.5 | 10.99 | -12.1% |
| Val loss | 12.6 | 11.00 | -12.7% |
| Cosine similarity | 0.72 | 0.75 | +6.1% |
| MSE | 11.2 | 10.5 | -6.3% |

6.1% cosine improvement with only 45 samples is meaningful for embeddings, especially because cosine similarity is harder to move than MSE—it requires actual semantic alignment, not just numerical proximity that could happen by chance.

### What the Model Learned

Structure preservation was the most surprising result: sidebar navigation held together across predictions, form fields remained recognizable as fields even when blurry, and the overall layout survived even when exact pixel values didn't match.

The model demonstrated compositional understanding in several ways: clicking "Create Deal" reliably led to form appearance, different action locations produced different predictions showing that positional encoding worked, and hover versus click produced different transitions showing that action type mattered semantically.

**The actual outputs:**

![Action - Typed "Testing 1" into the "Description" field](/images/posts/ui-world-model/action_type_1.jpg)

![Action - Typed "Type A" into "Subcategory" input field](/images/posts/ui-world-model/action_type_2.jpg)

These are reconstructions from predicted embeddings. Not perfect—text is blurry, colors drift—but the structure is there. You can see it's a CRM interface. You can see form fields. The model learned the layout dynamics, not just memorized pixels.

**Failure modes were predictable and mostly decoder-related:** fine text rendering came out blurry due to decoder limitations, color accuracy was inconsistent because embeddings don't prioritize exact color values, and rare transitions were poorly predicted due to insufficient data coverage.

## Why It Worked (Technical Analysis)

### Transfer Learning from V-JEPA

The 45-sample success comes entirely from V-JEPA's pre-trained representations, which already encode object boundaries and shapes, text regions that aren't OCR-accurate but are text-like, layout structure and spatial relationships, and temporal dynamics from video pre-training.

The world model only had to learn a simple mapping: given current state plus action, what's the next state? It didn't need to learn what a button looks like, because that was already encoded in the embeddings.

### Compositional Structure

The $[1, 2048, 1024]$ embeddings are compositional in structure, where each of the $2{,}048$ tokens represents a specific spatiotemporal region, the $1{,}024$-dimensional embedding captures rich semantics for that region, and the transformer attends across tokens to model relationships between regions.

This compositionality enables generalization across different scenarios, so the model learned that clicking a button in the top-right leads to a form appearing in the center as spatial relationships in embedding space, rather than memorizing specific pixel patterns.

### Action Conditioning

The action embedding, which combines type and coordinates, was essential for meaningful prediction, because without it the model would simply predict the most likely next state regardless of what action was taken, but with it, the model could distinguish between different outcomes: clicking at (0.85, 0.12) on the "Create Deal" button makes a form appear, hovering at (0.5, 0.3) makes a tooltip appear, and typing at (0.5, 0.5) updates a field.

Coordinate encoding enables spatial reasoning about where elements are located, while action type enables semantic reasoning about different interaction modes and their consequences.

## What This Enables (If It Matures)

**Planning before acting:** Like V-JEPA 2's MPC for robots, simulate multiple action sequences, choose optimal path. Plan in latent space (fast), execute optimal sequence.

**Error prevention:** Predict consequences before execution. Detect "this click will discard unsaved work." Intervene.

**Self-correction:** When predictions don't match reality, detect anomaly, backtrack, retry with modified parameters.

**Speed:** V-JEPA 2 plans at 16 seconds per step vs diffusion at 4 minutes per step. $15\times$ speedup. For UI automation: near-instant planning for complex multi-step tasks.

**Privacy:** Apple's UI-JEPA targets on-device operation at around $\sim 3\text{B}$ parameters for iPhone and iPad, which means no cloud calls and screen content never leaves the device.

---

## Limitations (Honest)

**Data:** 45 samples worked for this constrained task, but general UI understanding needs scale, and while Apple used 1,700 videos and Meta used millions, the sample efficiency here is good but not infinite.

**Action space:** I only modeled simple clicks and hovers, whereas real UI needs complex typing, scrolling, drag-and-drop, context menus, and multi-modal inputs, each of which requires extending the action embedding space.

**Generalization:** The model learned Zoho CRM's specific layout, and transfer to Salesforce, HubSpot, or IDEs remains untested, though V-JEPA embeddings generalize well, the dynamics model may not.

**Decoder:** The CNN reconstructed structure but not fine details, and a diffusion-based decoder like V-JEPA 2's visualization model might improve fidelity.

Current literature splits into two camps: **Model-based RL** (Dreamer, TD-MPC) uses world models for policy learning in simulation, offering strong planning but weak visual generalization, while **JEPA-style SSL** (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, UI-JEPA) uses latent prediction for representation learning, providing strong visual understanding but weak explicit planning.

What's missing is the union: latent world models for both representation learning AND planning in real-world UI environments.

This experiment is a small step that shows three things: V-JEPA embeddings capture UI structure, simple transformers can learn UI dynamics in latent space, and decoding back to visual space is possible.

The next step would be to add an explicit planning loop, using MPC in latent space for UI navigation, similar to V-JEPA 2's robotics work.

## Conclusion

45 training samples, one weekend, and off-the-shelf V-JEPA embeddings were enough for the model to learn UI state transitions in latent space and decode them back to recognizable visuals.

World models for UI are tractable, not just academic curiosities but practical alternatives to screenshot-hungry MLLMs, and Apple's UI-JEPA already shows $50\times$ compute reduction for intent prediction, suggesting that extending this to full state prediction could bring similar gains to action execution.

The field moves fast: V-JEPA 2 does zero-shot robot control, UI-TARS hits SOTA on GUI benchmarks, and the gap between research and production is closing rapidly.

For practitioners, the takeaway is simple: start with embeddings, not pixels, because latent space is where understanding lives, and pixels are just noise.

The future of UI automation isn't bigger vision models, it's smarter world models that understand why interfaces behave the way they do, not just what they look like.
